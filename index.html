A few things to try first

Most connection problems are caused by the TV being asleep, the phone using a different Wi‑Fi network, or a device-specific setting.

01

Keep devices on the same Wi‑Fi

Connect your iPhone and TV or media device to the same home network. Guest Wi‑Fi and some mesh or router settings can keep devices from seeing each other.

02

Wake Fire TV before pairing

Turn on the Fire TV and leave it awake. In Remotoking, retry pairing and watch the TV for a pairing prompt. Enter the on-screen PIN in the app.

03

Allow local network access

On iPhone, check Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network and make sure Remotoking is allowed. Then return to the app and find devices again.

Common questions

Why can’t Remotoking find my TV?

Make sure the iPhone and TV are on the same local network and that local network access is enabled for Remotoking. Some TVs require their network-control setting to be enabled. Discovery and controls vary by TV model and software.

Why didn’t a Fire TV pairing prompt appear?

Wake the Fire TV first, then tap Retry connection in Remotoking. If it still does not appear, open the Fire TV remote on the TV, wait until it is ready, and retry. The TV and iPhone need to be on the same network.

Why might volume or power behave differently?

Volume, power, and input switching depend on the TV model and settings. HDMI‑CEC can help compatible connected devices coordinate power and input behavior, but support varies by setup.

Can Remotoking control every console or media player?

No. A room can include named input shortcuts for connected devices, while direct remote control depends on whether that device is supported. A shortcut can ask a compatible TV to change input without controlling the console itself.
